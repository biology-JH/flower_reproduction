# Website outcomes

## 1. Core flower anatomy and structure functions
- [x] Create a responsive, interactive insect-pollinated flower explorer labeling all ten requested structures: sepals, petals, stamens, filaments, anthers, carpels, style, stigma, ovary, and ovules. Selecting or hovering a structure reveals its precise function. Preserve the biological distinctions: stamens comprise filament + anther; carpels comprise stigma + style + ovary; ovules are inside the ovary. Use the supplied flower diagrams/images where suitable.

## 2. Core wind-pollination structures and pollen types
- [x] Directly compare wind-pollinated anthers and stigmas with insect-pollinated structures, including exposed, loosely hinged/versatile anthers on flexible filaments and large, branched, exposed feathery stigmas that catch airborne pollen. Contrast insect-carried pollen (large, heavy, rough/barbed or sticky) with wind-borne pollen (small, dry, smooth, lightweight; produced in large quantities). Make the differences visually scannable and accurate.

## 3. Core pollination and fertilisation
- [x] Display the exact pollination definition: transfer of pollen grains from an anther to a stigma. Distinguish pollination from fertilisation. Show the fertilisation sequence and state that a pollen nucleus fuses with the egg/ovule nucleus inside an ovule to form a zygote. Add a short accessible animation/timeline; honor reduced-motion preferences.

## 4. Core structural adaptations matrix
- [x] Provide a filterable/sortable comparison of scent, nectar, petal colour/size and the position/exposure of reproductive organs for insect- and wind-pollinated flowers. Allow students to sort or filter comparisons without losing biological context.

## 5. Core seed germination requirements
- [x] Build an interactive panel for all three required conditions—water, oxygen, and a suitable temperature—with toggles/sliders and a clear outcome indicating germination only when all three conditions are suitable. Explain water activates enzymes and expands cells, oxygen enables aerobic respiration, suitable temperature supports enzyme activity. Clearly note light and soil are not required germination conditions.

## 6. Supplement self- and cross-pollination flows
- [x] Clearly badge Supplement material. Explain and visualize self-pollination as pollen transfer to the same flower or another flower on the same plant. Explain and visualize cross-pollination as pollen transfer to a flower on a different plant of the same species. Show the source flower, pollen movement, recipient flower and plant boundary; do not conflate cross-pollination with simply moving to another flower on the same plant.

## 7. Supplement population effects comparison
- [x] Provide a balanced, interactive self-versus-cross matrix comparing genetic variation, resilience to environmental change, and reliance on pollinating vectors. Explain self-pollination can ensure reproduction without vectors or a mate but generally yields less variation; cross-pollination introduces new allele combinations and may improve population resilience, while success can rely on wind/insect vectors. Avoid implying every offspring is adapted or cross-pollination always guarantees survival.

## 8. Supplement pollen tube sequence
- [x] Present a multi-step visual sequence from compatible pollen landing and germination on the stigma, through pollen tube growth down the style, into the ovary and through an ovule's micropyle, to nucleus fusion. Keep pollen tube entry separate from the core definition of fertilisation and visually explain the connection.

Select the best suitable photographs and diagrams embedded in the user-supplied PowerPoint `16.3Sexualreproductioninplants.pptx` and use those instead of the former Word-document pictures.
